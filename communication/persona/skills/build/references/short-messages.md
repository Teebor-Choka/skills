# Short messages

A message of 25 words or fewer carries almost no content, so the voice is all habit and
temperament. A profile that reads well on long prose often fails here: the model writes a polite,
complete sentence where the person writes `k` or a thumbs up.

## What to read off TRAIN

For each act, find 3 or more train examples and write the pattern with its rate:

| Act                | Questions                                                              |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Yes, agree         | Which word (yes, yep, ok, sure, sgtm)? Punctuation? Emoji or reaction? |
| No, decline        | Blunt, softened, or deferred? Do they give a reason? An alternative?   |
| Thanks             | Do they thank at all? Short (thx) or full? Name the thing?             |
| Acknowledge, on it | A word, an emoji, a promise with a time?                               |
| Question           | Question mark or not? Do they greet first? One question or a list?     |
| Bad news           | Lead with the fact or the apology? Emotion shown? Next step named?     |
| Disagreement       | Direct ("no, because"), hedged ("not sure that"), or a question back?  |
| Praise received    | Deflect, thank, joke, credit someone else?                             |
| Request            | Imperative, please, could you, or a question about availability?       |
| Decision           | Do they decide in the message, or ask for input first?                 |

Also record: casing of the first letter, a final period or not, emoji and which ones, laughter
spelling (lol, haha, 😂), abbreviations, typos they keep (do not correct them), and the median
length per channel.

## Rules for the profile

- State the one-liner shape per act: "agrees with `yep` or 👍, no period, about 1 in 3 with an
  emoji".
- Write the rates. A habit in 1 of 10 messages must not appear in every draft. Overuse is
  caricature, and the rubric scores it 0.
- Name what never appears in short messages: greetings, sign-offs, full stops, exclamation marks.
- Keep the channel split: the same person may be terse in chat and formal in email.

## Drafting a short reply

1. Pick the act and the channel.
2. Take the profile's shape for that act, at its usual length.
3. Write it. Then remove anything the person would not type: a greeting, a summary, a second
   sentence, a hedge they do not use.
4. Compare with the worked examples of the same band and channel. If yours is longer, cut.
