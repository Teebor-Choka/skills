# Slide Construction Patterns

_Designing individual slides: visuals, animation, typography; and the slide antipatterns to avoid._

## Patterns

**Defy Defaults** (P) — Never accept tool/organizer default settings; differentiate to build identifiable style. How: don't pick first theme; start blank only if strong visual sense; customize forced corporate/conference templates (move Floodmarks, tweak font within family); if compelled, confine defaults to Bookends/Intermezzi away from content; beg forgiveness over permission. See: Floodmarks, Analog Noise.
**Analog Noise** (P) — Imperfect, unpolished visuals (handwritten fonts, film grain, uneven lines, monochrome, jitter) add interest and draw attention; study shows hard-to-read fonts boost retention. How: use noisy fonts (both tools supply them; PowerPoint lacks noisy-line generation, use 3rd-party); noisy stroke on Keynote shapes via Graphic Inspector; low noise as subtle highlight; noisy lines signal uncertainty/messiness. Avoid: overuse becomes irritant — use sparingly OR consistently (inconsistent use is glaringly obvious). See: Defy Defaults, Unifying Visual Theme.
**Á la Carte Content** (P) — Poll audience to build impromptu agenda; multiple choose-next points tailor content, distill to most-desired form when time-short. How: hyperlink "home" slide of category icons/text to section starts, link back at each section end (ungroup grouped elements in PowerPoint to link); variants — Talklet chunks, Build-Your-Own-Adventure ordering, tag cloud; state per-section time up front. Avoid: requires large surplus material; don't let demos slip into Dead Demo; unshown content needn't be mentioned (see Going Meta). See: Talklet, Coda.
**Composite Animation** (P) — Layer 2+ simple tool animations on one object simultaneously, creating a novel effect tools seem incapable of. How: place element, add build-in/entrance animation, copy element exactly atop original, change copy's animation to a compatible one, repeat per layer; pick animations that differ visibly (not left-wipe + typewriter — too similar). Avoid: govern motion; novelty is the point — exception: reuse same composite to flag same recurring emphasis. See: Emergence. Note: richer/smoother in Keynote (e.g. shimmer+convergence+flame) than PowerPoint.
**Foreshadowing** (P) — Plant subtle clues leading to a later revelation, building toward shared epiphany. How: scatter peripherally-related items with strong metaphorical connection to what's foreshadowed; explicit (literal placeholders, distinct from an agenda) or implicit; longer gap between clue and payoff = bigger impact (people forget). Avoid: easy to overuse — pick 1–2 anchor points; confusing symbolism backfires. See: Narrative Arc.
**Vacation Photos** (P) — Full-screen high-quality images with few/no words; audience can't read pictures so verbal focus stays on presenter, who carries the full verbal channel. How: source stock (iStockphoto) or Creative Commons (Flickr; check license); use same-set/same-feel images as theme; plot placement via Light Table, winnow to highest-impact. Avoid: images must clearly fit (mismatched ones become a puzzle); don't linger on one image; beware clichéd "smiling lady" stock; secure copyright. See: Unifying Visual Theme, Photomaniac.
**Coda** (P) — End-of-deck list of further reading/references, not spoken in delivery; keeps sidebars out of Narrative Arc flow while aiding follow-up. How: usually a bibliography (no prescribed citation style); bullets and low artistic flair acceptable here; keep a reusable template. Avoid: demands extra accuracy/prep. See: Infodeck, Vacation Photos, Á la Carte Content.
**Greek Chorus** (P) — Seed audience with partisans who interject comments, show enthusiasm, or defend your case when outnumbered — evens the playing field for controversial/status-quo-defying talks. How: bring friends (Posse); or use no extra people, just extra personas — line-art alter ego / character on slides making recurring parenthetical comments; or stuffed animals. Avoid: not a stunt/plant asking staged questions — backlash if audience senses it. See: Posse, Mentor.
**Peer Review** (P) — Sparse, terse, clear, grammatically correct, objective slide writing; have a literate colleague/editor critique the draft. How: escalating polish — (1) spell/grammar-check, add jargon to dictionary; (2) read aloud; (3) sharp-eyed friend reviews; (4) docs/marketing team; (5) paid copyeditor. Avoid: no complete sentences on slides (invites reading over listening; signals you want an Infodeck); avoid passive voice (hides the actor, sounds unsure) — except when deliberately obscuring responsibility. See: Leet Grammars, Tower of Babble.

## Antipatterns

**Bullet-Riddled Corpse** (AP) — Slides that are dense bullet lists (sometimes columns), little more than speaker notes read aloud; text and speech compete, audience reads every word. Fix: bullets suit an Infodeck, not a presentation — differentiator is time; break outline ideas apart and introduce slowly; if unavoidable, at least use a reveal animation or Charred Trail; prefer Fourthought planning. See: Cookie Cutter, Ant Fonts, Injured Outlines, Fourthought.
**Cookie Cutter** (AP) — Letting tool-dictated slide capacity drive content: padding blank space with insipid filler or cutting wanted info to fit one slide lets the medium alter the message. Fix: no rule that one idea = one slide; slides are free, use as many as needed; convey the idea concisely across words + visuals; hide slide boundaries with Soft Transitions (dissolve transition + entrance animations; omit title animation on continued slide so title "stays"). See: Soft Transitions, Fourthought.
**Ant Fonts** (AP) — Shrinking fonts (30pt default down to 14pt or below) to cram content; unreadable, harms vision-impaired, leaves no verbal space, hides what matters. Slide size is arbitrary with no relation to proper content size; never say "can't read this in back" — you've failed the audience; no bonus for fewer slides. Fix: template with big default fonts; disable PowerPoint auto-shrink; Fowler's HalfSize Composition (design at 50% zoom — too small there = too small projected); code ~18pt (Inconsolata font). See: Bullet-Riddled Corpse, Slideuments.
**Fontaholic** (AP) — Using many font faces (four/six/nine, even three in one paragraph) to "jazz up" slides; disharmonic, jarring, hard to read, a hangover for the audience. Fix: pick mostly one sans-serif (Helvetica/Helvetica Neue, Arial, Verdana; FHWA series legible at distance); don't match fonts to pictures unless a pro typographer. See: Photomaniac, Floodmarks.
**Photomaniac** (AP) — Random stock photos/clip art with only specious connection to the topic, dumped to fill space or fake liveliness; makes the talk feel random. Fix: choose images matching the Unifying Visual Theme, use sparingly, buy sufficient resolution (low-res pixelates, feels cheap), credit Creative Commons photographers; break monotony with Talklet/Brain Breaks instead; drop the closing handshake photo. See: Unifying Visual Theme, Vacation Photos, Bullet-Riddled Corpse.
**Floodmarks** (AP) — Extraneous repeated background branding on every slide (logos, sponsors, swooping lines, copyright notices, watermarks); numbing noise that eats canvas and forces images to be shrunk/overlapped. Fix: keep templates Floodmarks-free; confine any mandated branding to Bookends (first slide), then revert to Unifying Visual Theme; refusing them implements Defy Defaults and makes you stand out. See: Defy Defaults, Bookends, Fontaholic.
**Injured Outlines** (AP) — Bullet lists are outlines; abusing outline rules — chiefly a heading with only one sub-item — signals unrefined thinking; every heading/subheading needs ≥2 parts, stay consistent (all sentences or all phrases). Fix: never a single sub-bullet at an indent level; a lone bullet should just become the title (enlarge it centered); plan before touching the tool; resolve orphans or break the rule consciously for effect. See: Cookie Cutter, Fourthought, Bullet-Riddled Corpse.
**Borrowed Shoes** (AP) — Presenting someone else's work/slides; your style imprints on your materials, so another's deck never finds the right rhythm — usually a terrible talk. Fix: best, rework into your own style; else reserve extra practice (Carnegie Hall, Crucible) and add a Narrative Arc to inherited fact-dumps; if too jarring, present minimally/whiteboard as an announced stand-in; if given a bad deck and no time, decline or delay so the owner delivers it. See: Carnegie Hall, Crucible, Narrative Arc.
