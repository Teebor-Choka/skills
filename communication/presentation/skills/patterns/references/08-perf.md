# Performance Patterns

_On-stage delivery techniques that make a talk land._

**Carnegie Hall** (P) — Only real prep for the unexpected is practice; rehearsing also reveals what talk is truly about. How: say it aloud early (even to pets/coworkers), record a dry run, chase Hiccup Words. Do 4 real rehearsals: 1st for content/pacing/timing, 2nd for delivery/appropriateness, 3rd auditions fixes + ingrains material, 4th finds the groove. Present in realistic settings (user groups); reuse material to amortize polish. See: Crucible, Narrative Arc, Hiccup Words.
**Stakeout** (P) — Arrive early — no excuses. How: leave slack for traffic; if venue not campable, stage at nearby coffee shop with cushion so chaos can't make you late. On arrival scout room, restrooms, organizer/AV, projector+audio; watch stage edges; know how to mute lav mics (assume always live). See: Seeding Satisfaction, Bunker.
**Shoeless** (P) — Small creature comforts ease the speaker despite odd looks. How: remove shoes, wear favorite undershirt/sneakers/comfy clothes, favorite beverage, deliberate laptop placement, step from lectern to mingle. See: Breathing Room, Carnegie Hall.
**Weatherman** (P) — Speak to the audience even when gesturing at screen; never turn your back. How: stand to the side like a TV weatherman, glancing between screen and crowd; use unmirrored heads-up display (current+next slide, timer, notes, red/green transition bar); use glossy-screen reflection to watch animations while facing crowd; can subtly interact with screen (break fourth wall). Avoid: neck-craning to use wall as monitor — use Lipsync or briefly re-mirror for demos. See: Make It Rain, Soft Transitions, Lipsync.
**Lightsaber** (P) — Minimal, thoughtful laser-pointer use as on-demand teaching tool for on-the-fly highlights. How: use green (color-blind can't see red), clicker with built-in laser, hold dot still — don't swivel wildly. Avoid: overuse (turns to rock concert, ignored, hurts low-vision/color-blind) — err toward underuse; recurring highlights → use Traveling Highlights instead. See: Traveling Highlights, Laser Weapons (AP).
**Breathing Room** (P) — Purposeful silence lets concepts settle; don't fill every moment. AKA Accordion, Padding. How: plan slightly-longer pauses at emphasis points (nerves make you rush + reach for Hiccup Words); mark them in speaker notes rather than trusting memory. Avoid: rushing — speaking too fast cuts comprehension and signals nervousness. See: Know Your Audience, Narrative Arc, Brain Breaks, Shoeless.
**Emotional State** (P) — Read the audience's mood/condition and adapt; springboard off positive feelings. How: gauge frame of mind via social networks, Twitter, conference blogs, organizers, fellow speakers, speakers' room/dinner; modulate tone, jokes, content accordingly. See: Social Media Advertising.
**Entertainment** (P) — Use humor, stories, analogies to hook and convey message — season lightly, don't drown message. How: structure stories like film (hook → climax/twist → resolution → restate point); keep short and tied to Narrative Arc; humor mid-talk and at end (or opener); tasteful self-deprecation humanizes. Avoid: overuse (golden hammer — audience won't know when you're serious), off-color/culture-dependent jokes (can become Alienating Artifact) — when in doubt, leave out. See: Know Your Audience, Crucible, Brain Breaks.
**Mentor** (P) — Play mentor, not hero — the audience is the hero; guide/tutor without leading by the nose. AKA Tutor, Knowledge Gifts, Gradual Revelations. How: analyze audience knowledge level, ensure all content serves what they should gain; fight curse of knowledge by simulating ignorance; recall order of your own epiphanies to guide theirs; may reorder material (e.g. high-level first). See: Narrative Arc, Display of High Value.
**Make It Rain** (P) — Props (candy, paper airplanes, chairs) add flair and draw out preternaturally shy groups. How: plan or improvise props that drive a point home; reward good questions/answers with treats to tap competitive instincts; scout room for co-optable objects. Avoid: don't put an eye out throwing candy. See: Entertainment.
**Seeding the First Question** (P) — Plant an obvious question early to break Q&A ice. AKA Warming Up, Breaking the Ice, Kickstarting. How: seed a doubt/question anywhere in talk (e.g. promise to reveal a technique like Preroll at end); once someone breaks ice, questions flow. Obviousness doesn't reduce effectiveness. See: Preroll, Greek Chorus, Posse, Know Your Audience, Display of High Value.
**Echo Chamber** (P) — Always repeat and paraphrase an audience question before answering. AKA Repeat the Question! How: make it a habit for every question — confirms you heard, buys thinking time, summarizes ramblers, eliminates duplicates, keeps answer in context for all. See: Carnegie Hall, Crucible.
**Red, Yellow, Green** (P) — Colored feedback cards dropped in a bucket on exit for instant, high-participation talk voting (red=bad, yellow=fair, green=good). AKA Quick Vote, Straw Poll. How: place card stacks + bin at each exit, explain at talk's end, monitor tallies; coarse 3 options force people off the fence. Avoid: little actionable detail (no comments); "green shift" bias when presenter sees votes; slows room egress on short breaks. See: Seeding Satisfaction, Crucible.
