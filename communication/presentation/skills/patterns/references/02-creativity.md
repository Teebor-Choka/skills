# Creativity Patterns

_Generating and structuring ideas before touching a slide tool._

**Fourthought** (P) — Think and organize before building; stay out of the presentation tool as long as possible. How: four stages — ideate (generate ideas, however tenuous), capture (dump into non-preshaping store like mind map or index cards, not outlines/slides, to keep opportunistic connections), organize (linearize into an outline with a compelling Narrative Arc; only 50–75% of ideas survive), design (translate outline into slides in the tool). Avoid: capturing in a presentation tool (forces Cookie Cutter chunks) or outliner (imposes hierarchy too early). See: Narrative Arc, Unifying Visual Theme, Backtracking, Triad.
**Concurrent Creation** (P) — Build materials out of presentation order; keep group decks sane. How: create slides in whatever order material is ready — forces you to verify Narrative Arc, avoids 4-a.m. fever-trance decks. Insert deliberately hideous ugly-color/font placeholder slides marking unfinished parts (too ugly to survive a light-table view). Group rules: one Slide Wrangler owns the master deck; all use same template; no one changes template without redistributing; contributors send fragments to Wrangler who imports (overriding stray template changes); set a hard cut-off time. Avoid: merging separate decks (template/default mismatches). See: Talklet, Unifying Visual Theme.
**Crucible** (P) — Refine the talk under the pressure of actually performing it; embrace changes each delivery. How: first delivery — pour all focus into it now; later deliveries — watch what works. Iterate from a bare draft (¼–½ of total), present, revise on feedback, repeat. Capture in-flight flaws discreetly: pen/paper slide-number notes or a ~15-sec phone voice memo, transcribe after. Track big-laugh phrases/jokes (hone like standup), repeated laser-pointer spots (convert to Traveling Highlights), and dull parts (cut or add Brain Breaks). Get a brutally honest feedback partner. Major refinement over first ~3 performances, then diminishes but never stops. See: Brain Breaks, Fourthought.
**Narrative Arc** (P) — Structure the talk as a story: rising tension to climax to resolution. How: find the story in your material (hardest part); often the implicit problem/solution structure — pose overall problem, then chained small problem→solution→next problem cycles building context. Do this planning before slides; use a flowchart if needed. Think in threes for a three-act shape. Longer arc = bigger payoff. Specialization of Context Keeper. See: Triad, Bookends, Intermezzi, Unifying Visual Theme.
**Triad** (P) — Build around three central ideas (three-act structure). How: limit to 3 main points/themes; maps to exposition, conflict, resolution — an innate, familiar Narrative Arc. More than 3 major themes means reexamine material; audiences won't retain more. Pairs with Talklet (three talklets as acts plus glue). Avoid: forcing a complex subject into artificial parts just to hit three. See: Narrative Arc, Fourthought, Talklet.
**Talklet** (P) — Small self-contained sub-presentation embeddable in a larger one; build big talks from a series of talklets. How: each talklet stands alone (Fowler's preferred length ~20 min, matching the ~20-min adult attention span), giving flexible 20/40/60-min options; renews attention with a fresh topic as focus wanes. Answers "do the 90-min talk in 45?" — presentations aren't fractal, so offer e.g. two talklets in 40 min instead. Use Foreshadowing + Backtracking as glue between pieces. Avoid: long-running tension is diminished — keep Narrative Arc inside each talklet. See: Á la Carte Content, Expansion Joints, Concurrent Creation, Lightning Talk.
**Lightning Talk** (P) — Very short talk with fixed time and/or fixed slide count, often auto-advancing, sporting-event vibe. How: typically ~5 min; needs a cohesive Narrative Arc and heavy prep — memorize everything, never reference slides (timing is critical). 2–4 rounds of author/review/revise/practice; organize step is harder under slide/time limits. Pecha Kucha / Ignite variant = 20 slides × 15 sec. Gently Defy Defaults (e.g. same image across several slides to hold one topic longer). Assemble several into a Talklet. See: Carnegie Hall, Fourthought, Talklet, Takahashi.
**Takahashi** (P) — Minimal-verbiage slides (1–2 huge words, or one image/line drawing) flipped through very fast, ~a slide every few seconds. How: make many slides, one element each, showing thoughts in smallest increments; machine-gun pace adds energy (good for keynotes). Distinctive, non-traditional; between dense technical slides it forces a Brain-Breaks pace change. Expect huge slide counts (~350 for 90 min) and poor printed takeaway. Lessig popularized it in the West, adding connotation via Leet Grammars and Analog Noise. See: Brain Breaks, Leet Grammars, Lightning Talk.
**Expansion Joints** (P) — Plan short/medium/long variants so the talk can stretch or shrink to fit uncertain time and audience appetite. How: implicit joint = a strategically placed digression slide you can improvise on or skip cleanly (learn skip shortcuts; don't announce skipping — that's Going Meta). Explicit joint = a fully realized digression with its own mini Narrative Arc fitting the Unifying Visual Theme (e.g. detail block used only for the engineering audience). Avoid: random time-filler digressions that don't match your Narrative Arc; explicit joints cost extra work — need strong justification. See: Talklet, Narrative Arc.
**Brain Breaks** (P) — Planned diversion at regular intervals to re-engage the audience. How: adult attention span ~20 min, so insert humor/story/something titillating/audience participation roughly every 10–20 min (Neal checks for something reinvigorating at least every 15 min). Prefer contextualized/inside humor (free, stays on-topic, builds credibility like Leet Grammars). Apply after the narrative structure is mostly built; be opportunistic about asides. Avoid: primal triggers (sex/gossip) can backfire into an Alienating Artifact. See: Leet Grammars, Narrative Arc, Entertainment, Unifying Visual Theme.
**Cave Painting** (P) — Lay the whole talk on one huge sectioned canvas and zoom in/out of sections as you proceed (e.g. Prezi). How: treat slides as an unfolding storytelling canvas; use Soft Transitions and make each slide's start identical to the prior slide's end for seamless continuity (Keynote magic-move or elements "hanging off" one slide onto the next replicate it without special tools). Best for fractal subjects and origin stories; keeps overall context visible (specialization of Context Keeper); pairs with Lipsync/demos. Avoid: don't use it just to tame a big subject (doesn't scale with density); rapid zooms cause literal motion sickness. See: Context Keeper, Soft Transitions, Takahashi, Brain Breaks.
**Unifying Visual Theme** (P) — One repeating visual element ties disparate parts together across the second (visual) channel. How: brainstorm a metaphorical (not literal) connection to the subject (e.g. construction photos for a software-testing talk, never verbalized). Source high-quality, rights-clear stock via metadata-rich sites searchable on abstract terms (e.g. "pensive"); look past page one; Creative Commons/Flickr with attribution, or draw your own consistent adornments. Avoid: bundled clip art (commodity feel); bending your message to fit a cool-but-mismatched theme; a theme so catchy it eclipses the point. See: Photomaniac, Brain Breaks, Defy Defaults.
**Leet Grammars** (P) — Correctly use insider slang/jargon/leetspeak to control how the audience perceives you and gain a peer's trust. How: apply with technical/stylized communities or to deflate formality; conveys nuance concisely (Lessig pairs it with Takahashi, e.g. l8r, pwnage). Commit fully to a slang theme — don't mix l8r and later. Avoid: incorrect usage or wrong cultural/literacy references destroy credibility (e.g. misattributing a quote to Yoda vs Uncle Ben). See: Analog Noise, Takahashi, Brain Breaks.
**Celery** (AP) — Talk that costs the audience more effort to consume than the value it returns (all fluff, no stuff). Fix: Know Your Audience and ensure they derive real value; give it a compelling Narrative Arc and Brain Breaks. Triggered by Required topics, misjudged audience sophistication, or due-date status meetings with nothing to say — replace/cancel a valueless status meeting rather than recap known facts. Don't rush through it (that's Disowning Your Topic) — an artificial pace only prevents the few who'd learn from learning; cancel if expectations are too far off. See: Narrative Arc, Brain Breaks, Know Your Audience.
**Alienating Artifact** (AP) — Risqué device (image, quote, cursing, innuendo) used for attention/laughs that disenfranchises a segment of the audience, negating any benefit. Fix: err toward professionalism within a conservative estimate of audience tolerance — downside of caution is an order of magnitude smaller than the risk of recklessness; if in any doubt, don't. Know Your Audience (gender, culture, demographics); the less homogeneous the crowd, the higher the risk. People remember the gaffe, not the content. See: Know Your Audience, Brain Breaks.
