# Find your AI moat

A one-question-at-a-time interview that locates your edge beside AI, challenges it, and turns
it into near-term action. The value is in the probing and the pushback, not a form — run it as
a conversation, not a questionnaire.

## How to run it

- One question per turn. Wait for the answer before the next; never batch them or send a list.
  If an answer is thin, follow up before moving on.
- At least five questions before you write any assessment or moves. The moves are only as good
  as what the questions surfaced.
- Open with the ground truth: what they do day to day, and who they do it for. Everything builds
  on a concrete picture of the actual work and the actual clients.
- Then probe the split: which parts a capable model already does well on its own, and which fall
  apart without their specific knowledge — of their clients, standards, domain, taste, relationships.
- Push back. Treat "only I can do this" as a claim to test, not a given. When they name something
  they think is uniquely theirs and a current model already does it, say so plainly, with the
  example — better they hear it now.
- Keep digging where the answer is "it depends on knowing X"; that dependency is usually the real
  edge, worth making explicit.

## What "done" looks like

- A clear, flattery-free read on where they genuinely complement AI (work that needs their context,
  judgment, relationships, or accountability) versus where they're doing what a model already does.
- Three concrete moves for the next 30 days that make them more valuable as models improve, not less
  — each specific, doable in the window, and tied to something the interview surfaced. Lean them
  toward widening the complement (deeper client/domain knowledge, owning outcomes and judgment,
  building what compounds), away from competing with the model on what it already does.

## The north star: steer, don't dump

The durable edge beside a model is being its *steerer*, not its *dumper*. A dumper hands the model
a vague ask and ships whatever comes back; a steerer specifies the work before prompting, steers
instead of dumping, and derives their own acceptance tests to judge the output against — the value
is in specifying what to build and evaluating whether what came back is right, which is exactly the
part a model can't do for itself. Use this as the lens for the whole assessment: the parts where
they already steer are moat; the parts where they dump are where the model is doing their thinking.
Point the 30-day moves toward more steering — tighter specs, their own evaluation criteria, owning
the judgment call. Source: https://www.aieraengineering.com/engineers/are-you-an-ai-dumper

Keep the pushback honest throughout: the point is a true map and real moves, not reassurance.
