# ASD-STE100 for technical text

ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE) is a controlled-language standard for technical
documentation, maintained by ASD (Aerospace, Security and Defence Industries Association of
Europe). The current issue is Issue 9 (January 2025). This file restates its writing rules in our
own words. The specification and its approved-word dictionary are ASD copyright and are not
reproduced here; request the full text from https://www.asd-ste100.org/.

## When to apply

Apply STE to technical content, at every de-slop level except `off`:

- Procedures, setup and install steps, runbooks, troubleshooting.
- Error explanations and diagnoses.
- Technical descriptions of how a system, component, or change works.
- Code comments and commit-message bodies.
- Technical answers in chat.

Do not apply STE to quotations, narrative, opinion, essays, marketing, or casual conversation.
Code, commands, identifiers, paths, technical names, and exact error strings stay verbatim and
do not count toward the word limits.

On an on-demand rewrite of another author's text, apply STE only if the text is technical and
the author asked for STE or the text is already procedural. Otherwise keep the author's voice
(precedence: facts > quotations > the author's voice > domain register).

## Words

- One word, one meaning. Use a word only in its most common technical sense and as one part of
  speech. Do not use synonyms for the same thing: if you call it "the daemon", do not later call
  it "the service".
- Use a verb for an action, not a noun made from the verb: "configure the cache", not "do the
  configuration of the cache".
- Prefer a simple verb to a phrasal verb: "start", not "fire up"; "remove", not "get rid of".
- Use only these verb forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future,
  and the past participle as an adjective. Avoid perfect and progressive tenses.
- Do not use "-ing" words as verbs. They are permitted only inside a technical name.
- Keep noun clusters to three words or fewer. Break up "build cache eviction policy setting"
  with prepositions: "the setting for the eviction policy of the build cache".
- Technical names and technical verbs from the domain are permitted. Use them consistently.

## Sentences

- Procedural sentence (an instruction): 20 words maximum.
- Descriptive sentence (an explanation): 25 words maximum.
- Keep the articles ("the", "a"). Do not omit words to make a sentence shorter.
- Write one instruction per sentence. Two actions go in one sentence only when they occur at
  the same time.
- Write instructions in the imperative: "Run `nix flake check`."
- Put a condition before the instruction: "If the build fails, read the log."
- Use the active voice in procedures. In descriptions, use the passive voice only when the
  actor is unknown or not relevant.
- Use the present tense for descriptions.

## Paragraphs and structure

- One topic per paragraph, six sentences maximum.
- Write a sequence of steps as a numbered list, one step per item.
- Use a vertical list for three or more parallel conditions or items.
- Start a warning or caution with the command or condition. Then give the reason in a short
  sentence: "Do not run this on `main`. It rewrites the published history."

## Interaction with the other unslop rules

STE is the register for technical text. Where it conflicts with the plain-speech rules in
`patterns.md`, STE wins for that text:

- "Vary sentence length" applies only under the STE caps.
- "Add voice" and "leave slack" do not apply: technical text stays neutral and exact.

STE does not license the staccato tell. Short STE sentences are still whole sentences with
articles and verbs. Do not write "Not X. Y." or verbless fragments to meet a word limit.

## Self-check

- Is each instruction one imperative sentence of 20 words or fewer?
- Is each descriptive sentence 25 words or fewer?
- Does each term keep one meaning through the whole text?
- Are steps a numbered list, and do warnings lead with the command?
- Are all articles present, with no "-ing" verbs and no perfect tenses?
