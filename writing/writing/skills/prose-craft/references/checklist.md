# The ordered pass

Twenty checks, run as three sweeps in order: structure and coherence, then sentence
architecture, then word-level concision. Each item is one imperative plus how to check it by
hand. No tooling. Provenance is tagged `[S&W]` (Strunk and White) or `[P]` (Pinker); the
reasoning behind each is in `canon.md`.

Run top to bottom. The order carries most of the value: a paragraph you are about to
restructure is not worth polishing at the word level first.

## Tier A: structure and coherence (whole passage)

1. **One point per paragraph, stated at the top.** `[S&W]`
   Check: read only the first sentence of each paragraph. Do they alone form a coherent
   outline of the argument? If a paragraph has two points, split it.

2. **Every sentence serves its section's claim.** `[P]`
   Check: for each sentence, name how it advances the paragraph's point. If you cannot, cut it
   or move it.

3. **Given before new.** `[P]`
   Check: each sentence opens with information the reader already has and ends with what is
   new. If the new idea is buried in the middle, move it to the end.

4. **Consistent topic string.** `[P]`
   Check: read the grammatical subjects of consecutive sentences in a row. Do they track one
   entity, or wander? Keep the passage about one thing at a time.

5. **Explicit connectives between sentences.** `[P]`
   Check: between adjacent sentences, is the logical relation named (because, but, therefore,
   for example, despite this) or left for the reader to guess? Name it.

6. **Show something in the world; strip metadiscourse.** `[P]`
   Check: delete "in this section", "it is worth noting", "as we will see", "I would argue
   that". Does the meaning survive? Then the phrase was noise. Point at the thing, not at your
   act of writing about it.

## Tier B: sentence architecture

7. **Concrete actor as subject, strong verb, active voice.** `[S&W 11][P]`
   Check: is the grammatical subject a real agent doing a real action? Convert "was Xed by Y"
   to "Y Xed".

8. **Kill nominalizations.** `[P]`
   Check: scan for nouns ending -tion, -ment, -ance, -ity that hide a verb ("the
   implementation of the cancellation" → "we cancelled it"). Restore the verb.

9. **Keep subject and verb close; related words together.** `[S&W 16][P]`
   Check: count the words between the subject head and its verb. A long gap taxes the reader's
   memory; move the interrupting clause. Modifiers sit next to what they modify.

10. **Heavy material to the end; no deep nesting.** `[P]`
    Check: does a long clause or list sit before the main clause (left-branching)? Move it to
    the end. More than one interruption between a subject and its verb (center-embedding) means
    flatten it.

11. **Every pronoun resolves to one nearby, named antecedent.** `[P]`
    Check: for each it/this/that/they, is there exactly one thing it can refer to, close
    by? If two, name the noun.

12. **Coordinate ideas in parallel form.** `[S&W 15]`
    Check: items in a series or a contrast share grammatical structure ("running, jumping, and
    swimming", not "running, jumping, and to swim").

13. **Break loose and/but/so chains; prefer coordination over deep nesting.** `[S&W 14][P]`
    Check: no run of independent clauses strung on conjunctions. Can a nested monster become
    two sentences without loss? Then make it two.

14. **Emphatic word at the end.** `[S&W 18]`
    Check: the word you most want to land is the last content word of the sentence. The stress
    position is the end; do not waste it on a qualifier or a trailing prepositional phrase.

## Tier C: word-level concision and precision

15. **Omit needless words.** `[S&W 13]`
    Check: remove each word or phrase in turn. If the meaning survives, it stays out. Kill "the
    fact that", "in order to", "there is / there are … that".

16. **Definite, specific, concrete over general and abstract.** `[S&W 16][P]`
    Check: for every abstract or category noun ("the device", "the process", "the relevant
    factors"), could a reader point at its referent? If not, name the thing or add an example.

17. **Statements in positive form.** `[S&W 12]`
    Check: replace "not honest" with "dishonest", "did not remember" with "forgot". State what
    is, not what is not, unless the negation is the point.

18. **Cut qualifiers and intensifiers.** `[S&W]`
    Check: delete very, rather, quite, somewhat, pretty, really. Does the sentence weaken? It
    usually sharpens. Strunk called these "the leeches that infest the pond of prose".

19. **Plain word over jargon; gloss any needed term of art.** `[S&W][P]`
    Check: for each elevated or in-group word, is there a plainer exact equivalent? If a term
    of art is unavoidable, define it at first use or cut it.

20. **One tense in a summary; consistent register throughout.** `[S&W 17]`
    Check: no unmotivated shift between past and present, or between formal and casual.

## The escape hatch

These are strong defaults, not gates. Optimizing for zero flags produces choppy, dumbed-down
prose. Break any rule sooner than write something wooden, and hedge only the spots that are
genuinely uncertain rather than every sentence. Judgment sits above the list.
